I open-sourced my AI SDR

Of everything I've automated, this is the build I'm most proud of: CleverResponder, a fully functional AI SDR that handles campaign replies end to end — classify, draft, approve, send, follow up. I built it twice, once for SmartLead and once for Instantly, and both workflows are free on my GitHub. Here's how it works, node by node.

The problem: replies are where campaigns go to die

Cold outreach tools are great at sending. The trouble starts when someone actually answers. Replies arrive at all hours, in every shape — "interested, send more info", "book me a call", "not interested", out-of-office bounces, and the occasional message that defies categorization. An SDR now has to read each one, figure out what it is, write a response in the company's voice, and follow up if the prospect goes quiet. Response speed matters enormously here, and it's precisely when the team is busiest that replies sit unanswered the longest.

That grind is what CleverResponder removes. Not by letting an AI run wild in the inbox — by giving the boring 80% of replies a fast, safe, supervised path, so humans only touch the messages that need them.

What it does, end to end

  • Receives every campaign reply through a webhook, the moment it lands.
  • Classifies it — interested, info requested, meeting requested, not interested, out of office, and more.
  • Drafts a reply with AI, grounded in a knowledge document of FAQs and company details — so answers come from your facts, not the model's imagination.
  • Asks a human — the draft lands in Slack with approve/disapprove buttons. Nothing sends without a click.
  • Sends and follows up — approved replies go out; meeting requests get a calendar link, and if no booking happens within a few days, the workflow checks in and nudges.

The first thing to notice: the AI is not the first stop. A rules-based router splits replies before any model gets involved, because not every reply deserves AI. Someone asking for a meeting doesn't need a clever paragraph — they need a calendar link, immediately. Someone saying "remove me" needs exactly that, not persuasion. Only the replies that genuinely need a written answer flow to the AI, which drafts grounded in a Google Doc of FAQs the client controls. Change the doc, change the answers — no retraining, no redeploying.

My favorite part is the tail end. After a calendar link goes out, the workflow waits four days and checks: did they book? If not, it asks the team in Slack whether to nudge — and once a meeting is booked, it even sends a "good luck" message before the call. Small touch, but it's the difference between automation that feels robotic and automation that feels like a colleague.

The Slack approval gate

The load-bearing node in both workflows is n8n's sendAndWait Slack step. The AI's draft posts to a channel with two buttons, and the workflow literally pauses until a human clicks. Approve, and it sends. Disapprove, and the team gets a notification that this thread needs a manual reply — the automation steps aside.

This is the pattern I'd recommend to anyone building AI into a business process: let the AI do the writing, let a human keep the veto. You get 90% of the speed with none of the "the bot said what?!" risk.

The Instantly build

Porting the idea to Instantly forced some new engineering. Instantly's webhook doesn't hand you the full lead context, so the workflow first reconciles the reply against the campaign by UUID — with a wait-and-retry loop for when the lookup isn't ready yet. Real integrations are full of these small timing quirks, and handling them is the difference between a demo and something that runs unattended.

This version also adds a redaction pass — a cleanup step between the AI draft and the approval message that polishes tone and strips anything that shouldn't go out. And the follow-up logic became a proper loop: three days after a meeting-related reply, it checks whether to remind, asks in Slack, sends the reminder — and comes back around until the meeting books or the team says stop.

Grab the workflows

Both builds are open source — each repo has the complete n8n workflow JSON you can import directly, plus the canvas export and a README:

  • SmartLead-AutoResponder — webhook → rules router → AI draft → Slack approval → send → meeting reminders.
  • Instantly-AutoResponder — UUID reconciliation → AI classify & draft → redaction → Slack approval → send → 3-day follow-up loop.

To run one: import the JSON into n8n, connect your SmartLead or Instantly account, Slack workspace, and AI credentials, point the knowledge-doc node at your own FAQ document, and start with every reply routed through the approval gate. Earn trust category by category before you loosen anything — I wrote more about that discipline in my practical guide to email automation with n8n.
