GST return working sheet

The arithmetic you would otherwise do in a spreadsheet on the tenth of the month: the invoices added up rate by rate, CGST and SGST separated from IGST, B2B split from B2C, exports kept out of the domestic buckets. Then the two questions a scrutiny notice opens with — is there a hole in your invoice series, and is any number used twice. It checks. It does not file.

  • Rate-wise totals, the shape GSTR-1 wants
  • CGST and SGST split from IGST
  • Real GSTIN check-digit validation
  • Invoice gaps and duplicate numbers
  • Nothing uploaded, nothing filed

Before you start

This tool runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you paste or drop here is uploaded, logged or stored — which for security work is the whole point, not a footnote.

What it does not do

  • It files nothing and reaches no GST portal
  • No uploads — there is no server to upload to
  • No account, no history, nothing saved
  • It cannot tell you a GSTIN is registered, only well-formed
  • Capped at 6 MB a file, 40 files, 20,000 CSV rows

Why that matters here

  • Your customer list never leaves this tab
  • GSTINs and turnover you paste are never transmitted
  • Nothing you paste ever appears in your Network tab
  • Works with no internet once the page is cached
  • Free, permanently

How it decides what it decides

        

        

          

            
The split comes from two state codes

            
A GSTIN begins with the two digits of the state that issued it, so the supplier’s state is read straight off the number you type in — the page says which state it read and where it read it from. That code is compared against the place of supply: the same state means CGST plus SGST at half the rate each, computed separately rather than halved after rounding; a different state means IGST at the full rate. In a union territory without its own legislature the state half is UTGST, which the sheet names. If either code is missing the document is listed as undecided rather than guessed at.

          

          

            
The CSV it reads

            
If you write your invoices in the invoice maker, skip the CSV: drop the invoice-data.json it downloads and every field here is already filled in. Otherwise — a header row is required and column order does not matter. Four columns are needed: date, invoice_no, taxable_value and rate. Six more are optional: doc_type (invoice, credit_note, debit_note), customer, customer_gstin, place_of_supply (a two-digit state code), tax, and supply_type (domestic, export_lut, export_with_tax, sez_lut, sez_with_tax). Common aliases are accepted — invoice_date, party_gstin, taxable_amount and so on. Several rows may share one invoice number, so an invoice with a 12% line and an 18% line is read as one document with two rate lines.

          

          

            
What the checks actually catch

            
A missing serial inside a run of invoice numbers, and a number used twice in the same series and financial year. GSTINs that fail the real base-36 check digit, or whose state code, PAN shape or entity code is wrong. Tax that is not the rate times the taxable value, beyond a one rupee tolerance. Dates outside the period you chose, dates outside the financial year, and dates the parser could not read at all. Each one is listed with the document it came from, so you can go and look.

          

          

            
What it deliberately does not compute

            
Input tax credit, reverse charge on inward supplies, advances received and adjusted, amendments to earlier periods, TDS and TCS credits, compensation cess, interest and late fee. Every one of those needs figures this page has never seen, and a 3B liability built from output tax alone would be wrong in your favour — the expensive direction to be wrong in. Nil-rated, exempt and non-GST supplies all land in the 0% row together, and GSTR-1 wants them apart.

          

        

      


      

        

          
Questions people ask

        

          

            Does this file my GST return?
            
No. It is a working sheet to check your own numbers against before you file. It does not file anything, it is not connected to the GST portal, it has no login and it makes no network call of any kind. It is also not a substitute for your accountant, and GST rules change with every finance act.

          

          

            Is my invoice data uploaded anywhere?
            
No. The JSON files are read with a FileReader and the CSV never leaves the textarea. Everything is arithmetic in this tab. That matters here more than on most pages: this input is your entire customer list, your turnover and your buyers’ GSTINs.

          

          

            It says a GSTIN is valid. Does that mean the buyer is registered?
            
No. A passing checksum means the number is WELL-FORMED: fifteen characters, a state code in 01 to 38 plus 97 and 99, a PAN-shaped middle, an entity code, the Z, and a check digit that matches the first fourteen characters. Whether the registration exists, is active, or belongs to whoever gave it to you can only be answered by the GST portal, and this page never contacts it.

          

          

            It found a gap in my invoice numbers. Is that a problem?
            
A gap is not proof of anything. Cancelled invoices leave gaps, so do numbers reserved and never used, a second series kept in another book, and documents you simply have not exported here. It is worth knowing because it is the first thing a scrutiny notice asks about, and the answer is much easier to give while you still remember it.

          

          

            Why is the B2C large threshold a setting rather than a number in the code?
            
Because it changes. The threshold for reporting an inter-state B2C supply invoice by invoice moved from 2,50,000 to 1,00,000 rupees, and it can move again. The page defaults to 1,00,000, prints the figure it used at the top of every sheet, and lets you type a different one. The threshold applies to inter-state supplies only and is tested against the invoice value including tax; intra-state B2C is small whatever its value.

          

          

            What can I feed it besides the invoice maker’s export?
            
Any CSV with a header row carrying at least date, invoice_no, taxable_value and rate. Tab-separated pastes out of a spreadsheet are detected too. Rows it cannot read are reported by line number with the reason, rather than dropped quietly, because a row that vanishes from a tax working sheet is worse than a row that complains.
