GST invoice and quotation maker

Most free invoice generators print CGST and SGST on every document they make, whoever the buyer is. That is wrong roughly half the time: under GST the split is decided by comparing your state with the place of supply, and a supply that crosses a state line takes IGST instead. This one asks for both states, says out loud which rule it applied, and refuses to guess when either is missing. It is a document generator — it makes one invoice or one quotation and prints it. It is not accounting software and it is not tax advice.

The part every free invoice tool gets wrong

The place of supply decides the tax

Not your address, not the client’s billing address by itself — the place of supply. Same state as yours, and the tax splits into CGST and SGST at half the rate each. A different state, and it is IGST at the full rate. Print the wrong pair and both sides file a return that does not reconcile, which is why the tool shows the comparison it made in words above the form.

UTGST is not a typo for SGST

A union territory without its own legislature — Chandigarh, Lakshadweep, Ladakh, the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, and Other Territory — charges UTGST as the state half. Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir have legislatures, so they take SGST. The label follows your state automatically, because nobody should have to remember that list.

Paise, not floating point

Every amount is held as a whole number of paise from the first multiplication onwards, each line and each tax half is rounded once at a defined point, and the discount is allocated back across the lines so the shares add up to the discount exactly. Then the grand total is rounded to the nearest rupee and the difference prints as a round-off line, so the column still adds up by hand.

Four situations, four documents

Registered supplier: a tax invoice with the rate-wise summary a GST invoice needs. Export or SEZ under a Letter of Undertaking: zero rated, with the declaration printed and the rates still shown. Below the threshold: no GST at all and a plain invoice. Composition scheme: a Bill of Supply carrying the line about not being eligible to collect tax. The heading changes because the document genuinely is a different one.

How it works

Everything is local

There is no server behind this page, so nothing you type can be uploaded. The form, the four templates, the tax arithmetic and the preview all run in your browser. That matters here for a specific reason: an invoice carries your client list, your rates, your GSTIN and whatever payment details you put on it, and those are precisely the things a free tool with a server has an incentive to keep.

Drafts are a decision, not a default

“Save draft” writes the form into this browser’s storage on this device, and nothing else writes anything. It is never loaded back automatically — the page tells you a draft exists and waits, because a form full of a client’s details reappearing for the next person at a shared desk is not a feature. “Delete saved draft” removes it. “Download data” gives you the whole thing as a .json file you own and can carry anywhere.

Printing is the export

There is no PDF library here on purpose. Your browser already ships an excellent PDF engine — the print dialog — and the print rules hand it only the sheet, sized to A4, with the site header, the toolbar, the form and even the site’s own byline removed. Choose “Save as PDF” and you have the document; choose a printer and you have paper. No watermark either way, because there is no step at which anyone could add one.

What it is not

It is not accounting software: no ledger, no numbering series, no tracking of what has been paid, no returns. It is not tax advice either — it cannot tell you which rate your work attracts, whether you should be registered, or whether a supply is zero rated. If you need that, ask a chartered accountant. Questions people ask

Does anything I type leave my browser?
No. There is no server behind this page, so there is nowhere for it to go. Your client list, your rates, your GSTIN and whatever you type into the payment details box are held in the page while the tab is open, and the only copies that ever exist are the ones you make on purpose: a printed PDF, a downloaded .json file, or a draft you save into this browser. There is also no share link anywhere in this tool, deliberately - a link that carried the form would carry the payment details with it, into browser history and into whatever app you sent it through.
Is this accounting software, or tax advice?
Neither. It is a document generator: it lays out one invoice or one quotation and prints it. It keeps no ledger, tracks nothing across documents, files no return, and cannot tell you what rate your work attracts or whether you should be registered at all. Those are questions for a chartered accountant, and the arithmetic here does not replace one. What it does do is the part software should never get wrong - the tax split, the rate-wise summary and the totals.
How does it decide between CGST plus SGST and IGST?
By comparing two state codes: yours, and the place of supply. Same state means the supply is intra-state, so the tax splits into CGST and SGST at half the rate each. Different states means inter-state, so it is IGST at the whole rate. If your state is a union territory without its own legislature - Chandigarh, Lakshadweep, Ladakh, the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu, or Other Territory - the state half is UTGST rather than SGST. Delhi, Puducherry and Jammu and Kashmir have legislatures, so they take SGST. Until both states are set the tool refuses to pick, because guessing is exactly how the wrong tax gets charged.
What if I am not registered for GST?
Switch the tax treatment to "not registered" and the tax columns disappear, no GST is charged, and the document prints as a plain invoice with a line saying no tax has been charged. If you are under the composition scheme, choose that instead: it prints as a Bill of Supply and carries the declaration a composition taxable person is required to print, because a composition dealer may not collect tax on supplies. The heading changes with the mode for the same reason - only a registered person issues a tax invoice.
How do I get a PDF?
Press "Print / Save as PDF" and choose "Save as PDF" as the destination in your browser's print dialog. The page is set up so that only the document prints - no site header, no toolbar, no form, no site byline - on A4 with the sheet carrying its own margins. There is no PDF library on this page on purpose: your browser already has an excellent one, and nobody can put a paywall in front of your own print dialog.
Where do my bank details go?
Into a plain text box, onto the printed document, and nowhere else. The tool never asks for an account number as a structured field, never validates one, never puts one in a link, and never sends one anywhere. If you save a draft, that text is part of what is written into this browser's storage on this device - which is why the draft is saved only when you press Save, and why there is a button to delete it. On a shared machine, print the PDF and skip the draft.
Will my draft still be here tomorrow?
Only if you pressed "Save draft", and only in this browser on this device. A saved draft survives closing the tab, and the page tells you it is there rather than loading it for you - a form full of client details and payment terms should not reappear on its own for whoever opens this page next on the same computer. "Load draft" brings it back, "Delete saved draft" removes it. If you want a copy you can move or keep, press "Download data" and you get an invoice-data.json file you own.
Why does it say my GSTIN is wrong?
A GSTIN is fifteen characters - a two-digit state code, the ten-character PAN of the holder, an entity number, a fixed Z, and a check character computed from the other fourteen. The check character catches the ordinary typo that a length check cannot, because a transposed pair is still fifteen characters long. The tool also says so when the state code at the front does not match the state you picked. None of it blocks printing: it is your number, and the warning is there to be read, not obeyed.
Can I bill in dollars for an export?
Not in this version - every amount is in rupees, and I would rather say so than print a dollar sign in front of arithmetic built for INR. The export mode does the other half of the job: it charges no tax, prints the zero-rated declaration for a supply made under a Letter of Undertaking, and leaves the rate column showing what would otherwise have applied. For a foreign-currency invoice you need the conversion rate and the date it was taken, which is bookkeeping, not layout.
Why is there a round-off line?
Because tax at 18 percent on an odd number almost never lands on a whole rupee, and the total on an Indian invoice is normally rounded to one. The tool computes every amount in paise, rounds the grand total to the nearest rupee, and prints the difference as its own line so that the column can still be added up by hand and reconciled. A total that quietly differs from the sum of the lines above it is the kind of thing an accounts department sends back.
