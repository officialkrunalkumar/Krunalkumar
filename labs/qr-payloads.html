vCard, Wi-Fi and UPI QR payload builder

A QR code is a string in a costume. This builds the string — contact cards, Wi-Fi joins, calendar events, map points and UPI payment requests — with the escaping most builders get wrong, and reads one back the other way. Paste a UPI code here before you scan it.

  • vCard 3.0, 4.0 and MECARD
  • Wi-Fi, calendar, geo and UPI
  • Escaping checked by round trip
  • UPI codes explained in plain words
  • No lookups, nothing uploaded

Before you start

This tool runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you paste or drop here is uploaded, logged or stored — which for security work is the whole point, not a footnote.

What it does not do

  • No uploads — there is no server to upload to
  • No account, no history, nothing saved
  • It cannot check that a UPI address exists or belongs to anyone
  • It cannot verify a sign= parameter — that needs a key it does not have
  • It does not decode EMVCo or Bharat QR — the kind that starts 000201
  • It does not draw the QR code; /labs/qr does that

Why that matters here

  • A Wi-Fi password you type is never transmitted
  • A payment address you paste is never looked up
  • No URL found in a payload is ever opened or resolved
  • Nothing you paste ever appears in your Network tab
  • Works with no internet once the page is cached
  • Free, permanently

A pay QR and a receive QR look identical. On UPI there is no such thing as a “receive money” QR — every UPI QR is a request for YOU to PAY. Anyone who tells you to scan a code to receive a refund is taking your money. Your UPI PIN is only ever needed to send.

The password goes into the code in clear text. There is no version of this format that hides it, so anyone who photographs the printed square keeps it forever. That is the argument for putting one on a guest network and nowhere else.

How many decimal places you keep decides whether this names a neighbourhood or a front door. The tool works the distance out for the latitude you give it.

Whatever you put in sign is copied through as text. This page cannot compute a signature and cannot check one — that needs a key issued by NPCI. Currency is fixed at INR because that is what UPI settles in.

What is actually inside the square

        

        

          

            
Every UPI QR asks you to pay

            
There is no “receive money” QR code on UPI. A payment request and a payment collection look the same because they are the same — a upi://pay URI naming a payee. If someone says scan this to get your refund, your prize, your deposit or your salary, completing it pays them. Your PIN is only ever needed to send. The parser here prints that on every UPI code it reads, including the ones you build yourself.

          

          

            
The payee name is just text

            
pn is a string typed by whoever printed the code. Nobody verifies it, and a code claiming to be from a bank costs nothing to make. The tool flags names that borrow authority and names carrying Cyrillic or Greek letters drawn like Latin ones, but it cannot check a UPI address against anything — that needs a lookup at NPCI, which is a network call this page will never make. Read the name your own app resolves, and stop if the two disagree.

          

          

            
Escaping is where builders break

            
vCard, MECARD and the Wi-Fi string all use punctuation as structure. An organisation called “Shah, Sons & Co; Security” contains two separators, and a builder that ignores them produces a card that arrives in pieces. Everything built here is escaped to the rule for its format, then parsed back and compared component by component, so you can watch the round trip rather than take my word for it.

          

          

            
A Wi-Fi QR carries the password in clear

            
The string is WIFI:T:WPA;S:name;P:password;; and the password sits in it as plain characters. Nothing about the format encrypts or hides it, so anyone who photographs the printed square keeps it forever, including people who were never in the building. That is not a reason never to use one — it is the reason the right place for one is a guest network that reaches the internet and nothing else.

          

        

      


      

        

          
Questions people ask

        

          

            Can I scan a QR code to receive money?
            
No. There is no such thing on UPI. Every UPI QR code is a request for the person scanning it to pay. A pay QR and a supposed receive QR look identical because there is only one kind. Anyone telling you to scan a code, or to enter your UPI PIN, in order to receive a refund or a prize is taking money out of your account. Receiving money on UPI never needs your PIN.

          

          

            Can this tell me whether a UPI address is genuine?
            
No, and it says so on screen every time. Checking that a VPA exists or belongs to anyone means asking NPCI, which is a network request, and this page makes none. What it can do is read the string: the shape of the handle, whether the amount is fixed or left for you, whether the payee name borrows authority or contains letters from another alphabet drawn like Latin ones. All of that is judged on the text, not on reality.

          

          

            Why does it not draw the QR code?
            
Because the QR encoder already exists at /labs/qr and there should be exactly one of it on this site. Two encoders means two things to check and two places for a bug to hide. This page produces the exact string; paste it into /labs/qr and that page draws the square, picks the mask and tells you which version it needed.

          

          

            What is line folding and why does it matter?
            
iCalendar and vCard say a line should not exceed 75 octets, and a longer one is split by a line break plus a single space. Almost nothing implements it, and the rule that gets missed most is that the split must not fall inside a multi-octet character. Skip that and a description in Devanagari or with an emoji arrives corrupted, silently. The folder here counts octets, never splits a character, and prints the length of every line so you can see it.

          

          

            Is it safe to put my Wi-Fi password in a QR code?
            
The password is inside the code in clear text and there is no way around that; the format has no room for anything else. Anyone who photographs the printed square has it permanently. On a guest network that reaches the internet and not the printer, the NAS or the cameras, that is a reasonable trade. On your main network it hands out the keys to everything on it.

          

          

            Does anything I type here leave my browser?
            
No. There is no fetch and no XHR in the code, no URL found in a payload is opened, resolved or previewed, and no address is looked up anywhere. That matters more here than on most pages, because the things people paste into a payload tool are Wi-Fi passwords, home coordinates and payment addresses.
