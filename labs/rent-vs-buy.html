Rent versus buy, compared on net worth rather than on the monthly outgo

Almost every comparison of renting and buying puts an EMI next to a rent and declares a winner. That comparison is not close to right: part of the EMI is principal, which is savings, and the renter has not thrown their down payment away either — they are still holding it. The only question with an answer is what each path is worth after the years you actually intend to stay. This models both, month by month, and marks the year the curves cross — or says plainly that they never do.

  • Net worth at year N, not monthly outgo
  • A real amortisation schedule, walked
  • The difference is invested, both ways
  • Crossover year, or none inside the horizon
  • Nothing you type leaves your browser

Before you start

This tool runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you paste or drop here is uploaded, logged or stored — which for security work is the whole point, not a footnote.

What it does not do

  • No uploads — there is no server to upload to
  • No account, no history, nothing saved between visits
  • No price data, no rent index, no lender or fund feed
  • No advice — it is arithmetic, and its limits are printed on the page

Why that matters here

  • A property price, a rent and a salary stay on your machine
  • Nothing you type ever appears in your Network tab
  • Every number on screen is computed in this tab, seconds before you see it
  • Works with no internet once the page is cached
  • Free, permanently

Two guesses decide this Everything here is arithmetic on numbers you type, and two of those numbers — property appreciation and investment return — are guesses that nobody can make reliably. Move each of them by a couple of points and the answer flips. That is not a flaw in the model, it is the finding, and the grid near the bottom of this page shows it happening. The starting values below are placeholders so the controls do something. They are not market data and they are not a recommendation. Replace all of them with your own.

The property and the loan
What owning costs beyond the EMI

Stamp duty and registration are state levies. There is no national rate to hardcode, they differ by several percentage points between states, several states charge a lower rate when the buyer is a woman, and the ready-reckoner value rather than your agreement value may be what the duty is charged on. Look up your own state and put its number here.

Renting, and what the money does instead

The renter does not simply pay rent. They still hold the down payment, the stamp duty and every rupee of the monthly difference, and this model invests all of it. It works both ways: in any month where owning costs less than renting, the buyer invests the difference at the same return. Model it in one direction only and the comparison is decided before it starts.

Selling at the end, and tax on the gains

Both gains toggles start off, and leaving them off is not neutral: it flatters the buyer and the renter in turn. Capital gains rules on property changed with a recent Finance Act, the indexation option depends on when you bought, and sections 54, 54F and 54EC can reduce a property gain to nothing if you reinvest. None of that is modelled here. Set the rate you believe applies to you, or leave it off and read the number as the pre-tax one.

Old-regime tax treatment — off by default

Read this before switching it on. Everything in this block belongs to the old income tax regime. The new regime is the default one, and under it there is no section 80C, no HRA exemption and no section 24(b) deduction on a self-occupied property. If you are in the new regime, none of this applies to you and the toggle should stay off. Every ceiling below is a field rather than a constant, because they move with each Finance Act and this page has no way to know which year you are in. Look yours up. This is arithmetic, not tax advice.

The two numbers nobody knows

          

            This is the honest output of the page. Property appreciation and investment
            return are each moved by up to four percentage points, every other field is
            held exactly where you set it, and the crossover year is recomputed from
            scratch in all twenty-five cells. If the answer were robust the grid would be

What this model cannot see

          

            The model prices cashflows. Most people do not decide this on cashflows, and
            the things that actually decide it have no number: whether you will still want
            to be in this city in six years, whether your parents will treat renting at
            forty as a failure, whether the flats available to rent near your work are
            places you would want to live, whether your landlord will renew, and what it
            is worth to you to never again be asked to vacate at two months’ notice.
            A house is also a forced savings plan, which for many people is the honest
            reason it works — a renter who spends the difference instead of investing
            it loses this comparison outright, and the model assumes they never do.
            It also leaves out prepayment, rate resets, loan top-ups, GST on
            under-construction property, possession delays, one-time society corpus,
            parking charges, and a market that does not move in a smooth annual percentage.
            Treat the output as arithmetic you can audit, not as an answer, and never as
            financial advice.
          

        

      


      

        

          
Four things this page exists to say

        

        

          

            
An EMI is not a rent, and comparing them is the mistake

            
The EMI contains principal, which is savings, and the rent contains none. So a rent of ₹22,000 against an EMI of ₹55,000 is not a ₹33,000 gap; part of that money is moving from one of your pockets to another. Compare the two paths on what you are worth at the end and the whole framing changes — which is why this lab plots net worth and nothing else.

          

          

            
The renter is holding the down payment, and it has to be invested

            
The down payment, the stamp duty and every rupee of monthly difference stay in the renter’s hands, and a comparison that lets that money sit at zero has already decided the answer. It runs the other way too: once the rent has escalated past the cost of owning, the buyer is the one with a surplus, and this model invests that as well. Both sides, or neither.

          

          

            
The costs that never appear in the brochure

            
Stamp duty and registration are state levies, not a national number, and they are gone the moment they are paid. Then maintenance and society charges, municipal property tax, insurance, and brokerage when you sell. This lab makes every one of them a field you set rather than a constant it invented, because the honest ones vary by state, city and building.

          

          

            
The answer turns on two numbers nobody knows

            
Property appreciation and investment return. Everything else in the model is a fact you can look up; those two are forecasts. Moving each by four points across a grid of twenty-five runs regularly turns a crossover in year five into no crossover at all. This page prints that grid instead of a verdict, because the grid is the true finding and the verdict would be a guess wearing a number.

          

        

      


      

        

          
Questions people ask

        

          

            Why compare net worth instead of the EMI and the rent?
            
Because the EMI and the rent are not the same kind of thing. Part of every EMI repays principal, which is savings you still own; rent buys you a month of shelter and nothing else. Meanwhile the renter is holding the down payment and the stamp duty. Net worth at the end of a stated horizon is the only comparison where both of those are counted, which is why this page computes that and refuses to headline the monthly figures.

          

          

            What stamp duty rate should I put in?
            
Whatever your state charges, which is why it is a field and not a constant. Stamp duty and registration are levied by the state, the rates differ by several percentage points across India, several states charge less when the buyer is a woman, and the duty may be assessed on the government ready-reckoner value rather than on the price in your agreement. This page has no way of knowing which state you are in, so it asks.

          

          

            Does it handle 80C, section 24(b) and HRA?
            
Yes, as an option that is off by default, and only for the old regime. Section 24(b) interest on a self-occupied property, 80C principal — sharing one ceiling with your PF, insurance and ELSS, and competing with the stamp duty in the year you buy — and the renter’s HRA exemption under 10(13A) as the least of its three tests, with the metro split. Every ceiling is a field, because they move with each Finance Act. The new regime removes all of it, so if you are in the new regime leave the toggle off. None of this is tax advice.

          

          

            Why does it not just tell me which one is better?
            
Because it does not know, and neither does anything else that claims to. The result is dominated by property appreciation and investment return, and those are forecasts. The sensitivity grid moves each of them by up to four points and recomputes the crossover year twenty-five times; the answer routinely flips inside that range. Printing one confident number on top of that would be dishonest, so the page prints the spread instead.

          

          

            Does it model prepayment, a rate reset or an under-construction flat?
            
No. There is no prepayment, no floating-rate reset, no loan top-up, no GST on an under-construction purchase, no possession delay and no one-time society corpus. Rent is assumed to be paid every month with no gap and no move. If you want to see what prepaying or a rate change does to a loan on its own, the loan and EMI lab handles all of that in detail.

          

          

            Is anything I type uploaded?
            
No. There is no server behind this page and no network request is made. The whole model — the amortisation, both portfolios, the tax arithmetic and the twenty-five sensitivity runs — is computed in your browser tab. A property price, a rent and a salary are not details worth handing to a website, which is exactly why the work happens here.
