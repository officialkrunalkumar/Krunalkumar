How it works

Everything is local, and nothing is left behind

There is no server behind this page, so nothing you type can be uploaded — the form, the templates and the preview all run in your browser. It is not written to this device either: the draft lives in the page’s memory for that one session, and closing or reloading the tab clears it. That is why there is no account — nothing for an account to unlock, nowhere for your work history to go, and nothing left on the library PC for the next person. It also means the draft is gone the moment you leave, so keep a copy on purpose: “Download data” hands you the whole thing as one small .json file — your resume as a file you own, ready to back up, reload here, or load on any other machine.

The templates are CSS, not images

Each of the five layouts is a stylesheet applied to the same document: your data is written into real HTML once, and switching template just swaps how it is arranged — single column, dark sidebar, banner band, serif centre, tight grid. That is why switching is instant and why the text in the final PDF is real, selectable text rather than a picture of text.

Printing is the export

There is no PDF library here on purpose. Your browser already contains an excellent PDF engine — the print dialog — and the page is set up so that printing produces only the resume, on A4 or US Letter, with the colours intact. “Save as PDF” in that dialog is the download button, and nobody can put a paywall in front of your own print dialog. If you have ever wondered why the paid sites charge at exactly that moment, I wrote up the resume-builder paywall trick in detail.

Every field is optional

The renderer only draws what you filled in. Skip the photo and the layouts close the gap; leave certifications empty and the heading never appears; give one job instead of three and nothing shows as missing. There are no placeholder boxes and no “complete your profile” nagging — an empty field simply does not exist on paper.

Questions people ask

Is it really free, with no watermark?
Yes. No account, no watermark, no paywall at the download step. The usual trick is to let you build the whole resume and then charge at the export button; this one cannot charge you there because the export button is your browser's print dialog.
Is my data uploaded anywhere?
No. There is no server behind this page, so there is nowhere to upload to. That matters for this particular form: name, phone, email, photo, and your entire work history are exactly the data resume sites collect and monetise. Here the form, the templates and the preview are all rendered in your own browser and nothing leaves it.
How do I get a PDF?
Press "Print / Save as PDF", and in the print dialog choose "Save as PDF" as the destination. The page is set up so only the resume prints — no site header, no buttons — on A4 with proper margins. Every browser has shipped this for years; it is the same engine paid sites use behind their download button.
Will my data still be here tomorrow?
No — and that is deliberate. Nothing you type is written to this device: the draft lives in the page's memory for that one session, so closing or reloading the tab clears it, and the next person to open this page on the same computer finds an empty form. Nothing to leave behind on a library PC, nothing for anyone to read later. The trade is that you have to keep a copy on purpose — press "Download data" before you leave and you get a resume-data.json file you own, and "Load data" brings it straight back. "Clear everything" empties the form on the spot.
Can I move my data to another computer?
Yes. Press "Download data" and you get a small resume-data.json file containing everything — every field, the photo, your chosen template, colour and paper size. Keep it as a backup, or copy it to another machine, open this page there and press "Load data". Your resume is a file you own, not a row in someone else's database.
Can I print on US Letter?
Yes. Flip the Paper toggle in the toolbar from A4 to US Letter and both the preview and the print dialog follow. A4 is the default because most of the world hires on it; switch to Letter if you are applying in the US or Canada, where it is the standard.
Does it work offline?
Yes. After your first visit the site's service worker keeps a copy of this page and its files in your browser, so it loads and prints with no connection at all. The tool never talked to a server in the first place, so once the page has arrived, nothing about it needs the internet.
Can I use my own photo?
Yes. Pick an image file and it is resized down in your browser — never uploaded — and placed into the templates that use one. Every template is also designed to look right without a photo, and for many corporate applications leaving it off is the safer choice.
Will it pass ATS scanners?
Use the Classic template for portals: it is a single-column layout with real selectable text and standard section names, which is exactly what parsers like. The fancier multi-column layouts — Sidebar, Banner, Compact — are for human readers, so send those as a PDF to a person, not to an upload portal.
