CTC to in hand, with every step shown

An offer letter gives you one large number and a table nobody explains. What actually reaches your bank account depends on how that number was split, whether the employer’s provident fund was counted inside it, which state you sit in, what rent you pay, and which tax regime you end up under. This takes it apart line by line, and shows the two or three places where the offer is genuinely negotiable.

  • Both tax regimes, side by side
  • Every slab, rebate, surcharge and cess
  • HRA as the minimum of three tests
  • Professional tax, month by month
  • Nothing you type is uploaded

Before you start

This tool runs entirely inside your browser. Nothing you paste or drop here is uploaded, logged or stored — which for security work is the whole point, not a footnote.

What it does not do

  • No uploads — there is no server to upload to
  • No account, no history, nothing saved
  • No tax advice — this is arithmetic you can check
  • No lookups against any external database

Why that matters here

  • Your salary and rent stay on your machine
  • A figure you type is never transmitted
  • Nothing you paste ever appears in your Network tab
  • Works with no internet once the page is cached
  • Free, permanently

Slabs for FY 2025-26 Everything below uses the rates for financial year 2025-26, which is assessment year 2026-27. Indian tax rules change with every Budget. Confirm the slabs, the standard deduction and the rebate against the current Finance Act before you use a number from here, and treat all of it as arithmetic rather than tax advice.

The HRA exemption, as its three tests

          
Section 10(13A) with rule 2A does not give you a
            percentage. It gives you three numbers and takes the smallest: the HRA you
            actually received, the rent you paid minus 10% of basic, and half your basic
            in a metro or 40% of it anywhere else. Which one wins tells you what to change
            — if the rent test is lowest, more HRA in the offer buys you nothing.
            None of this exists under the new regime.

          

        


        

          
Month by month, because the months differ

          
A single in-hand figure is an average, and the average
            is a month you never actually get paid. Professional tax lands unevenly in
            several states, the bonus arrives in one month and drags its tax across the
            months after it, and March absorbs whatever rounding is left over. This is the
            year as twelve separate numbers.

          

        


        

          
The one lever you can actually negotiate

          
The CTC is usually fixed long before you see it. The
            split is not. Basic drives provident fund on both sides, the gratuity accrual
            and the ceiling on your HRA exemption all at once, so moving it moves four
            things together. Drag the basic slider above and this table moves with it.

          

        

      


      

        

          
What a CTC is actually made of

        

        

          

            
Cost to company, not cost to you

            
A CTC is what the employer books as the cost of employing you, and it is allowed to include money that never reaches your account. The employer’s provident fund contribution, the gratuity accrual, sometimes an insurance premium and occasionally a notional cost for a laptop all count. Two offers with the same CTC can differ by a lakh a year in cash purely on what was loaded into it.

          

          

            
Basic is the number everything hangs off

            
Provident fund is 12% of basic on both sides. Gratuity accrues at 4.81% of basic. The HRA exemption is capped at 50% of basic in a metro and 40% outside it, and the rent test subtracts 10% of basic before it starts. One percentage point of basic moves all four. It is also the number employers are most willing to discuss, because it does not change the headline.

          

          

            
Special allowance is the balancing figure

            
Whatever is left after basic, HRA, the employer contributions and any bonus has to go somewhere, and that somewhere is usually called special allowance. It is fully taxable, carries no exemption of any kind, and exists so the components add up to the CTC. A structure with a very large special allowance is a structure with a very small basic.

          

          

            
Gratuity is accrued, not earned

            
The 4.81% is an accounting accrual. Nothing is payable until five continuous years are complete, so if you leave at three years that money was never yours. Counting it inside a CTC is entirely legal and entirely normal, and it is the line in the annexure most likely to be worth zero to you personally.

          

        

      


      

        

          
Where the two regimes actually differ

          
Both are computed above, on the same salary, with every slab shown. These are the differences that decide it.

        

        

          

            
The new regime is the default

            
If you say nothing, you are in it. Choosing the old one is an active declaration you make to payroll, and for salaried people the choice can be revisited each year at the time of filing. The new regime carries the larger standard deduction and the wider slabs; the old one carries the deductions.

          

          

            
The old regime is bought, not given

            
It only wins if you actually have the deductions. Rent that supports a real HRA exemption, a full 80C, health insurance, the extra NPS window, home loan interest. Add them up honestly before choosing it. People routinely pick the old regime on the strength of deductions they intend to make and then do not.

          

          

            
The rebate is a cliff with a rail on it

            
Section 87A wipes the tax out entirely up to a limit and stops dead above it. Without help, crossing that limit by a hundred rupees would cost tens of thousands, so the law adds marginal relief: just past the line, the tax cannot exceed the amount by which you crossed it. The tool shows the relief as its own line when it applies.

          

          

            
Surcharge is a tax on the tax

            
Above 50 lakh of total income a surcharge is added on top of the tax itself, stepping up at 1 crore, 2 crore and, in the old regime only, 5 crore. Each threshold has its own marginal relief for the same reason. Then 4% health and education cess is charged on the tax plus surcharge, which is why the last line is never a round number.

          

        

      


      

        

          
What this page does not know

          
It sees a CTC, a split, a rent and a handful of deductions. Everything here is outside that, and every one of them can move the answer.

        

        

          

            
The financial year, once it moves

            
The slabs, both standard deductions, the rebate, the surcharge bands and the cess all come from one Finance Act, and the banner at the top of the tool names which. They are set in a single object in the source, so updating the page is one edit — but until that edit happens, a Budget has moved and this has not. Check the year before you trust the number.

          

          

            
Everything that is not this salary

            
Interest income, capital gains, rent you receive, a second employer in the same year, arrears, perquisites, an employer NPS contribution under 80CCD(2), or a loss carried forward. Any of them changes the taxable income the slabs are applied to, and none of them are inputs here. This is one salary, computed cleanly.

          

          

            
Professional tax, at the resolution states use

            
Every state has its own slabs by monthly salary, and the figures here are the top slab, which is what applies at the sort of CTC this page is for. Someone on a lower salary pays less, or nothing. The constitutional cap is 2,500 rupees a year in every state, which is why no figure here can exceed it.

          

          

            
How your payroll actually behaves

            
TDS under section 192 only has to be right by March. How it gets there is the employer’s estimate, revised whenever you file a declaration or a bonus lands. The model here spreads the base tax evenly and recovers the bonus tax across the remaining months, which is common but not universal. Yours may front-load it, or catch up in February.

          

          

            
Which reimbursements are really tax free

            
The reimbursement field here is assumed non-taxable, which is a generous assumption. In practice it depends on the head, the bills you actually submit and the current position on each allowance. If you cannot produce the bill, treat it as salary, and move it into the special allowance field instead.

          

          

            
Anything about your own circumstances

            
Age, residential status, a disability deduction, section 80G, a joint home loan, or the year you switch regimes. I am a security engineer who reads Finance Acts carefully, not your accountant. The value here is that you can follow the arithmetic and argue with it, which is more than a single number lets you do.

Questions people ask

        

          

            Which financial year are these slabs for?
            
The year named in the banner at the top of the tool. That year lives in one place in the code, and the banner, the headings and the arithmetic are all rendered from it, so they cannot drift apart. Indian tax rules change with every Budget, so check the slabs, the standard deduction and the rebate against the current Finance Act before you rely on anything here. If they have moved, this page is out of date until I update it.

          

          

            Is this tax advice?
            
No. It is arithmetic, written out so you can check it rather than trust it. Your real liability depends on things this page never sees - other income, capital gains, a second employer in the same year, perquisites, an employer NPS contribution under 80CCD(2), arrears, or the declaration you gave payroll in April. For a number you are going to act on, talk to a chartered accountant.

          

          

            Why does my payslip not match this exactly?
            
Several small reasons that add up. Payroll usually posts the pension share of provident fund as a flat 1,250 rupees a month rather than 8.33 percent, applies professional tax on its own slab of monthly salary instead of the top rate, spreads TDS on a schedule of its own choosing, and may treat a reimbursement as taxable that this page assumes is not. The shape should match. The last few hundred rupees usually will not.

          

          

            Does anything I type here get uploaded?
            
No. There is no server, no account, and no analytics event that carries any figure you enter. A salary, a rent and a list of deductions together are close to identifying on their own, which is exactly why the whole calculation happens in this tab and why the page keeps working with the network switched off.

          

          

            Should I ask for a lower basic so I take home more?
            
Sometimes, and the sensitivity table shows you by exactly how much. A lower basic means less provident fund leaving your pay each month, but also a smaller employer contribution, a smaller gratuity accrual and a smaller HRA exemption if you are under the old regime. You are moving money between now and later, not creating any. Move the slider and decide which side you want it on.
