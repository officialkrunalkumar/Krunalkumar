Most typing tests measure you on ordinary prose, which is the easy case: common words your fingers already know as whole units. This one also measures you on real Python, JavaScript, C and SQL — the brackets, semicolons and capitalisation that actually slow people down while they are writing code. Why code typing is its own skill

The keys are in the wrong places

Prose lives almost entirely on the home row and the letters around it. Code lives on the number row, the brackets, and the keys under your little fingers — {}, [], <>, ;, =>, ::. Those are the slowest keys on the board for most people, and code uses them constantly.

There is no autocorrect for a typo

A misspelled word in prose is still readable. A misplaced bracket is a syntax error, and a misspelled variable is a bug you will spend ten minutes finding. That is why accuracy here is counted strictly: in real work, a typo you fixed still cost you the time it took to notice.

What a good number looks like

Around 40 WPM on prose is average for an adult; 60–80 is comfortably quick. On code, expect roughly a third less — someone typing 70 WPM of prose often manages 45–50 on code. The gap narrowing over time is a better signal of progress than the raw number.

Practise what you actually write

The passages here are drawn from the languages the playgrounds run, so the symbols you practise are the ones you will type next. When you want to run something rather than retype it, the playgrounds are one click away.

Questions people ask

How is WPM calculated?
By the standard convention: one word is five characters including spaces, so WPM is the total characters you typed divided by five, over the elapsed minutes. That is the same formula every other typing test uses, which means your number here is comparable with the ones you have seen elsewhere.
Why is my code speed so much lower than my prose speed?
Because it is a different skill. Prose is common words your fingers already know as units; code is punctuation, symbols and exact capitalisation, with far more use of the number row and the keys under your little fingers. A gap of 30-40% is completely normal, and closing it is the point of practising on real code.
How is accuracy measured?
Correct keystrokes divided by total keystrokes. Fixing a typo does not restore the accuracy you lost, because in real work the mistake still happened and still cost you time. Backspacing itself is not counted as a keystroke, so correcting is not penalised twice.
Is my typing data sent anywhere?
No. Everything runs in your browser, and your best score is kept in this browser's local storage on your own device. The Labs storage panel shows exactly what is stored and clears it in one click.
Does it work on a phone?
It runs, but a touch keyboard measures something quite different from a physical one. The scores are only meaningful against your own scores on the same kind of keyboard.
Why does the passage show an arrow at the end of a line?
That is a newline you need to type. In the code passages the line breaks are part of what you are practising, so they are shown rather than left invisible.
