UPI and payment fraud, taken apart

Almost every one of these turns on a single confusion, and it is not a technical one: people believe that receiving money can require an approval. It cannot. A collect request is a bill, a QR code is an address to pay to, and the six digits are only ever a signature on a debit. Decide seven screens, read the six scripts that actually reach people here, and know what the first hour looks like before you need it.

The one fact everything here turns on

If somebody only ever remembers one sentence about UPI, it should be this one, and it is short enough to say down a phone line to a parent.

The PIN authorises a debit and nothing else

Six digits, entered on your own device, inside your own app. That is a signature on money leaving. There is no screen in any payment app, for any amount, under any circumstance, where entering it causes money to arrive. Receiving is passive: you are told afterwards, and you were never asked.

A collect request is a bill, not a gift

UPI lets anybody with your address raise a request against your account. It arrives as a prompt and it dies there unless you approve it. The amount is chosen by them and the reason line is typed by them, so a request can read “refund” and still be a demand for money. Most of the fraud in India lives inside this one feature.

A QR code is an address to pay to

It carries a payee and sometimes an amount. Scanning one and approving it can start a payment out of your account and nothing else. There has never been a QR code that pulls money in, which means a buyer who sends you a code “to receive the payment” is asking you to pay them, whether or not they know it.

The system is not what failed

Every case on this page worked exactly as designed — a debit needed the payer’s PIN on the payer’s device, and it got one. That is the uncomfortable part. Because the technical route is closed, the fraud goes through the person instead, which is why the defence is a habit rather than a setting.

Getting one wrong here is the cheapest place to get it wrong.

If it is happening right now

The first hour, in short

The full version, with the reasoning behind each step, is in the third panel above. This is the part worth being able to find without JavaScript, without scrolling and without reading anything else.

Stop paying, then call 1930

There is no unlock fee, no clearance charge and no refundable deposit that ends this. Hang up, then call the national cybercrime helpline on 1930. Behind it sits the system that pushes your complaint out to the banks and payment intermediaries in the transaction chain, so funds still in transit can be held.

File at cybercrime.gov.in and keep the number

The National Cybercrime Reporting Portal takes every category and gives you an acknowledgement number. Keep it — every later conversation with a bank, an officer or a court starts from that number. Then call your bank’s own fraud line separately, and put the dispute in writing as well as by phone.

Speed is the whole argument

Money is layered onward within minutes, not days. A hold only works on what has not yet been split, withdrawn or converted, so the first hour is worth more than the week after it. Late is still not pointless: reporting is what links your complaint to others against the same accounts.

And the honest part

Most of it is not recovered. A hold is not a refund either — frozen money is evidence in a live case, and getting it released generally needs a court order and months. Partial recovery is the common good outcome, and anyone promising you more in advance, for a fee, is running the second scam.

Questions people ask

Can someone take money out of my account just by knowing my UPI ID?
No. A UPI ID is an address, like an email address. Somebody who has it can send you money, or raise a collect request asking you for money. The request cannot debit you on its own - it arrives as a prompt on your phone and dies there unless you approve it with your PIN. What people describe as being emptied by someone who knew their ID is nearly always an approval they were talked into giving.
Do I ever need to enter my UPI PIN to receive money?
No. Not in any app, not for any amount, not to verify anything, not once. The PIN is a signature on a debit and it does nothing else. Receiving needs no approval because there is nothing to approve - the money is simply there, and you find out afterwards. If a screen wants your PIN, money is leaving.
Somebody sent me money by mistake and wants it back. What should I do?
Do not return it through their link, their QR code or their request. Tell them to raise it with their own bank, which is how a genuine misdirected transfer is reversed - on the sender's written request, with a reference number. Leave the amount untouched, tell your own bank what happened, and keep the messages. If the money that reached you was itself stolen, passing it on by hand makes your account a link in the chain, and it is your account that gets frozen.
Are the payment screens on this page real?
No, and they are drawn so they could never be mistaken for one. Each carries a standing ribbon saying it is an illustration, no field on it accepts input, and the bar that looks like a confirm button is a paragraph of text with nothing behind it. There are no real UPI handles, no bank names and no logos anywhere on this page, and it makes no network request of any kind.
Will I get my money back?
Often not, and I would rather say that than imply otherwise. Your complaint triggers holds down the chain of accounts the money passed through, and only the portion still sitting somewhere in that chain can be held - anything withdrawn in cash, converted or moved abroad is out of reach. A hold is also not a refund: frozen money is evidence in a live case, and releasing it to you generally needs a court order and months of waiting. Partial recovery is the common good outcome, and nobody can promise you more in advance.
What is the difference between calling 1930 and filing at cybercrime.gov.in?
1930 is the helpline, and it is what starts the hold - the complaint is pushed out to the banks and payment intermediaries in the transaction chain so funds still in transit can be stopped. cybercrime.gov.in is the formal complaint, and it gives you an acknowledgement number that every later conversation with a bank, an officer or a court will start from. Do both, in that order, and then call your bank's own fraud line separately.
A buyer showed me a screenshot of the payment. Is that proof?
No. A screenshot is an image, and images are trivially edited. The only thing that settles it is your own side: the message from your bank, or the entry in your own passbook or statement. Wait for that before handing anything over, however awkward the wait is, because the awkwardness of the wait is precisely what the script is built on.
Is UPI itself insecure?
Nearly everything on this page is social engineering rather than a technical failure. A UPI debit needs the payer's PIN, entered on the payer's own device, inside the payer's own app - which is a sound design, and it is why the fraud has to go through the person instead of around them. That is also the uncomfortable part: the system worked exactly as specified in every case here. Somebody was persuaded to authorise their own debit.
