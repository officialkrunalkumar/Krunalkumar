Frequently asked questions

How do we start?

With a free intro call — or just a message if you prefer. I listen to the problem first; if I am not the right person for it, I will say so and point you somewhere better.

How do you charge?

Whichever way fits the work — hourly, project-based, full-time/retainer, or per session for corporate training. Either way you get a written scope with a quote before any work starts — deliverables, timeline, and price. No meters running quietly in the background. Ongoing 1:1 coaching is separate and billed monthly through the mentorship program.

Will you access any device or account I bring you?

Only devices and accounts you own or are explicitly authorized to grant access to. I may ask for proof of ownership, and I decline or stop any work where that isn’t clear — no unlocking, bypassing, or monitoring of anyone else’s device, ever.

Do you work remotely?

Yes — remote-first, with clients across the US, France, Taiwan, and India. Where the work allows it, delivery happens in the open on GitHub so you can verify progress yourself.

Do you train teams, not just individuals?

Yes — corporate training is a separate service line. One team at a time, 10–40 people, remote or on site, in sessions from half a day to three days. It is quoted per session or per program rather than by the hour, and I build the material around the systems you already use after a short intro call — no stock deck. The mentorship program is the 1:1 version, billed monthly.

What topics can you train a team on?

The list is above — security awareness, Linux and systems security, automation and AI workflows, engineering practice, technical writing, and professional skills like communication and remote collaboration. Topics combine into a longer program, and anything not listed is still worth asking about: you will get either a yes or an honest no.

Have you run corporate training before?

Not under my own name — this is a new service line, and I would rather say so plainly. What it is built on: teaching engineers since 2021 through MountBlue, Springboard, Coding Ninjas, Correlation One, and Codementor, and six months as an Assistant Professor teaching operating systems and information security. I hold no instructor certification — none of the certificates I list is a teaching credential. If you need a vendor who has run this exact session two hundred times, that is a fair reason to hire someone else.

Can corporate training count as evidence for an audit?

Partly, and I would rather be precise than convenient about it. ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A 6.3 asks for information security awareness, education and training, and SOC 2 assessors usually map the same evidence to the common criteria. What they want in practice is a record of who attended, what was covered, and when — and that record comes with the session. India’s DPDP Act asks for reasonable security safeguards rather than naming training outright, so treat a session as support for that case, not a box it ticks. I am not an accredited training partner of any certification body, and I cannot promise an audit outcome.

What do attendees get after a training session?

The materials as delivered, a written summary of what was covered with the date and the hours, and a completion certificate for everyone who attended — each carrying a unique ID your auditor or HR team can check on the verification page without going through me. Company names are never published there, and any attendee who would rather not be listed gets their ID confirmed by email instead.

Is what I share with you confidential?

Yes. Anything you share for an engagement — code, data, business details — is used only for that engagement and shared with no one. The formal version is in the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy.

What if I’m not satisfied with the work?

The written scope defines what is delivered, and refunds are handled per the Refund Policy — advance payments for work not yet started are refundable in full.
